\begin{abstract}
We study sensor-level traffic flow forecasting on the LargeST San Diego road graph ($716$ sensors, $15$-minute bins, $2020$--$2021$).
Starting from course message-passing notation, we build an ST-GNN with fixed, adaptive, and hybrid adjacency and compare it to a temporal-only baseline.
On real PeMS flow, the temporal baseline attains the lowest test MAE ($21.15$); among graph models, adaptive adjacency works best ($26.82$), while a fixed road graph alone is weakest ($60.37$).
Learned message matrices align only loosely with the physical adjacency, which helps explain why na\"ive graph convolutions can hurt when the road topology mismatches traffic coupling.
\end{abstract}
