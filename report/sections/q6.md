## Q6(c) — Learned vs fixed graph (quantitative)

Quantitative comparison of \(A_{\mathrm{adaptive}}\) against the road network \(A\) is produced by:

```bash
python -m src.analyze_graph --config configs/stgnn_adaptive.yaml --also-hybrid
```

`outputs/graph_analysis.json` reports, per run:

- Mean row entropy of \(A_{\mathrm{adaptive}}\) (concentration of messages).
- Pearson correlation between learned weights and fixed edge weights on pairs where \(A_{ij}>0\).
- **Top-\(k\) overlap fraction** (precision-at-\(k\)): for each node, fraction of the \(k=20\) largest learned edges that are also road neighbours in \(A\) — \(\lvert \mathrm{TopK}_i \cap \mathcal{N}(i)\rvert / k\). This is **not** Jaccard.

## Q6(d) — Critical discussion

1. **Does better forecasting mean the learned graph is road connectivity?**  
   **No.** Predictive correlation and physical adjacency answer different questions. Our learned top-\(k\) overlap with \(A\) is only \(\approx 5\%\): the model often messages non-neighbours that co-move statistically. That can help (adaptive beats fixed) without recovering the map.

2. **Why might hybrid beat fixed / adaptive on some benchmarks?**  
   On **this** real SD run, hybrid (**MAE 28.16**) did **not** beat adaptive (**26.82**) or temporal-only (**21.15**); fixed was worst (**60.37**). In general, hybrid mixes a road prior with flexibility; here the prior hurts because \(\tilde{A}\) misaligns with flow coupling.

3. **Limitations of a static graph**  
   PeMS coupling changes with time of day, incidents, and weekends. A single \(A\) cannot represent rush-hour corridor direction or broken sensors. Future work: time-conditioned or directed graphs, or stronger temporal encoders (as our baseline suggests).
